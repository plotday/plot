Jackson Lamb — Slow Horses

Containment as a Way of Life

Jackson Lamb’s days don’t begin so much as continue. There’s no clean edge between sleep and work—just a dim kitchen, a kettle that’s been boiled twice already, and a phone that hasn’t rung yet, which is how he knows it eventually will.

The problem arrives sideways, as usual: a badly written intelligence brief that wasn’t meant for him, forwarded by someone who didn’t care enough to double-check the recipient. Lamb reads it once, then again more slowly, not for what it says but for what it avoids saying. By mid-morning he’s decided two things: someone’s lying, and someone else wants it to be his problem without admitting it.

He spends the day nudging pieces without moving himself—short calls, shorter instructions, an email drafted and never sent. He sends River out on an errand that sounds pointless and assigns Louisa to verify something that “doesn’t matter.” By evening, both have learned exactly what he wanted them to learn. Lamb, meanwhile, eats something unhealthy, ignores a message from a doctor he hasn’t listened to in years, and deletes a voicemail that hints at consequences.

Tomorrow there will be a meeting that isn’t officially a meeting, with people who won’t admit they needed him. If he does his job properly, nothing will happen. No headlines. No credit. Just another small disaster quietly strangled in its crib.

Lamb wouldn’t call it satisfaction. But he’ll sleep just fine.
