Kate Wyler — The Diplomat

Making Progress Look Inevitable

Kate Wyler’s calendar looks like a battlefield map: overlapping commitments, time zones collapsing into one another, decisions hiding in the margins. Her morning starts early, not out of discipline but necessity—there’s a proposal she believes in, and belief, she’s learned, isn’t enough.

The initiative itself is modest on paper: a cooperative pilot, carefully worded, deliberately small. What makes it dangerous is that it works. It threatens to succeed without permission, and that makes everyone nervous. Kate spends her day refining language until it disarms rather than persuades, coaching allies through conversations they’re pretending not to want to have, and quietly removing obstacles before they’re noticed.

Between meetings, she checks in with her husband, who is somewhere else doing something equally important and entirely incompatible with her schedule. The call is affectionate, clipped, unfinished. She eats standing up, rereads a memo that almost derailed everything last night, and rewrites the agenda for tomorrow’s roundtable so the hardest question comes last.

That evening, she walks home instead of taking the car, letting the cold air do what coffee no longer can. She thinks about the version of herself that once reacted instead of shaped, and about how much effort it takes to make progress look calm.

Tomorrow, if she’s done this right, people will leave a room thinking the idea was theirs all along.
