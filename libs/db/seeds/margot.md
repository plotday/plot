Margot Whitcombe — AFC Marlow (Post-Hank era)

Building the Future Without Losing the Present

Margot’s office is quiet in the morning, and she likes it that way. It’s the only part of the day that belongs entirely to her. She reviews match data with a practiced eye—not to assign blame, but to understand patterns. The men’s team is faltering, not collapsing, and that distinction matters. Panic would cost more than patience.

She meets Wes first, then Eli, separately. Different conversations, same destination. One clear adjustment. Ownership stays where it belongs. She offers support without rescue, authority without intrusion. It’s a balancing act she’s gotten very good at.

In the afternoon, her attention shifts forward. A women’s team. Not an announcement—yet—but groundwork. Feasibility, facilities, league requirements, finances. She frames it not as an expansion, but as a continuation of values the club already claims to hold. Between calls, she reviews early branding concepts, privately delighted by one she won’t show anyone yet.

There’s a moment, late in the day, where doubt sneaks in. Is this too much at once? She answers an email from a supporter who thanks her for making the club feel like it belongs to everyone, and the doubt quiets.

Tomorrow brings a board pre-brief and a match that will test the new approach. The outcomes may diverge. Margot knows that leadership means holding both realities at once.

She leaves the office knowing the future won’t arrive on its own. Someone has to make room for it.
